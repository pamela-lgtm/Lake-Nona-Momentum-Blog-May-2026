Lake Nona West: It's Finally Here

The most talked-about development in Lake Nona history is no longer a rendering. It's real, it's opening in phases starting this month, and it is going to fundamentally change daily life for everyone who lives, works, and rents in this community.

Lake Nona West is a 405,000-square-foot open-air lifestyle center built by Tavistock Development Company on 54 acres along Lake Nona Boulevard at Boggy Creek Road. It's been called the second-largest retail project built in Florida in the last five years. Named the 2024 Retail Deal of the Year by NAIOP Central Florida and recognized by CoStar as the nationwide Retail Deal of the Spring, this isn't a typical shopping center — it is a destination that reflects everything Lake Nona stands for: thoughtful design, walkability, sustainability, and quality.

"This is not just another shopping plaza. Wide shaded walkways, 30 original murals by Miami artist Cinthia Santos, pocket greens, an activity plaza, and 20-stall ultra-fast EV charging powered by Mercedes-Benz. Lake Nona doesn't do ordinary."

The Jobs Are Coming. 3,000 of Them.

Retail is exciting. Jobs are the foundation. And Lake Nona just landed one of the most significant corporate relocations in Orlando's recent history.

Siemens Energy — a global energy technology leader — announced in August 2025 that it will relocate its Orlando offices to Lake Nona Town Center. The company will occupy more than 242,000 square feet at Lake Nona's newest Class A office building, and the move will bring nearly 3,000 employees to the community by 2027. These are high-wage, high-skilled professionals — exactly the kind of residents and renters who look for quality long-term housing in communities like ours.

Think about what 3,000 new workers mean for the rental market in a community that is already supply-constrained. These are not seasonal workers. These are professionals relocating to Orlando's fastest-growing district — many of whom will be looking for exactly the kind of quality single-family homes that Verandah Properties manages.

And that's just Siemens. In March 2026, Orlando city commissioners unanimously approved the Dowden Central Community Development District — a nearly 380-acre new development zone designed to channel growth around Medical City and Lake Nona's town center. The plan focuses development into walkable, mixed-use nodes, with affordable housing requirements and transportation improvements built in. This is long-term infrastructure for long-term growth — exactly the kind of structural investment that protects and compounds property values.

Medical City: Still Growing, Still the Anchor

Lake Nona's 650-acre Medical City has been the economic engine of this community for years — and it's not slowing down. If anything, 2026 is a milestone year for healthcare in Lake Nona.

AdventHealth Lake Nona — described as the "hospital of the future" — is expected to open in November 2026. The 10-story facility will offer advanced services including men's health, bariatrics, neurology, cardiology, and orthopedics, with an incisionless surgical suite using ultrasound technology. The 25-acre campus will feature a lake promenade, event lawn, amphitheater, and retail and dining options. It will have 80 beds initially, designed to expand to 320.

A Community With a Soul

What separates Lake Nona from every other master-planned community in Florida isn't just the technology or the healthcare — it's the culture that has quietly taken root here. And 2025–2026 has been a breakthrough year for arts and community life.

The Lake Nona Arts Festival returned for its second year in February 2026 at Lake Nona Town Center, featuring 100 local and national artists competing for $25,000 in cash awards. In 2025, the inaugural festival welcomed more than 100 artists and offered them a global stage to showcase their work. It's becoming one of Central Florida's signature cultural events — and it lives here.

For the first time, the City of Orlando's annual Veterans Day parade came to Lake Nona in 2025 — one of the largest in Orlando's history — celebrating the community's deep connection to the Orlando VA Medical Center and the growing veteran population living here.

And now, Lake Nona West will add 30 original murals curated by Miami-based studio Chalk & Brush, founded by Cuban-born artist Cinthia Santos — an installation drawing from native Florida flora, wildlife, and the community itself. Lake Nona's public art program is not an afterthought. It is woven into everything.
